You are using a **weighted aggregation system**. The individual `kiki_bias` and `bouba_bias` numbers tell you *how strongly a specific answer pushes toward Kiki or Bouba*, while the `weight` tells you *how important that question is for measuring that specific personality trait*.

Here is how the math and logic work together so you can see why this approach is so powerful:

---

## 1. How the Biases and Weights Work Together

Think of it like calculating a weighted GPA:

* **The Bias (`kiki_bias` / `bouba_bias`):** Represents the **grade/score** for a specific choice (e.g., Selecting "Night Owl" gives a `kiki_bias` of 40 and `bouba_bias` of 78).
* **The Weight (`weight`):** Represents the **credit hours** (how much that question actually matters for that specific group).

### Example Calculation for Group 2 (Spontaneity):

Imagine a user answers 3 questions in **Group 2**:

| Question | Chosen Answer | `kiki_bias` | `weight` | Weighted Kiki Score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Q3 (Future Plans)** | Kiki Choice | 89 | 0.9 | $89 \times 0.9 = 80.1$ |
| **Q6 (Sleep Schedule)** | Bouba Choice | 40 | 1.0 | $40 \times 1.0 = 40.0$ |
| **Q19 (Rules & Routines)** | Kiki Choice | 70 | 1.0 | $70 \times 1.0 = 70.0$ |

**Step 1:** Multiply each score by its weight and sum them up:


$$\text{Total Score} = 80.1 + 40.0 + 70.0 = 190.1$$

**Step 2:** Sum the weights:


$$\text{Total Weights} = 0.9 + 1.0 + 1.0 = 2.9$$

**Step 3:** Calculate the Crisp Group Input (0–100 scale):


$$\text{Crisp Input for Spontaneity} = \frac{190.1}{2.9} = \mathbf{65.55}$$

---

## 2. What Gets Fed Into Your Fuzzy Logic System

By doing this calculation for all 4 groups, you get **4 crisp numerical inputs (between 0 and 100)**:

1. **`Group 1 Score`** = $78.2$ $\rightarrow$ Feeds into **Social Interaction FIS**
2. **`Group 2 Score`** = $65.5$ $\rightarrow$ Feeds into **Spontaneity FIS**
3. **`Group 3 Score`** = $82.0$ $\rightarrow$ Feeds into **Cognitive/Decision FIS**
4. **`Group 4 Score`** = $50.0$ $\rightarrow$ Feeds into **Life Drive FIS**

Each of these scores goes into your membership functions (`Low`, `Medium`, `High` or `Introverted`, `Ambiverted`, `Extroverted`) to run your fuzzy rules!

---

## 3. Why This System is Superior

1. **Eliminates Noise:** A low-importance question (like Pen vs. Pencil, `weight = 0.5`) won't overpower a major lifestyle question (like Life Decisions, `weight = 1.0`).
2. **Handles Both Biases:** You can calculate a separate **Crisp Kiki Input** and **Crisp Bouba Input** for each group if you want to feed two separate fuzzy inference pipelines.
3. **Scalable:** If you decide to add 10 more questions later, your Fuzzy Logic rules won't break—the weighted average cleanly normalizes everything back to a 0–100 range.
